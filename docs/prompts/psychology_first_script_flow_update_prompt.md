# Psychology-First Script Flow Update Prompt

## Purpose

Redesign the script-generation flow so the final script feels like a **psychological analysis video**, not a story, diary, motivational speech, or self-help essay.

Target:

> Psychological analysis that happens to use everyday situations.

NOT:

> An everyday story with psychology added to it.

---

## 1. Core Principle

**The situation is NOT the topic.**

The psychological pattern behind the situation IS the topic.

Example:

### Bad
"Why do you become anxious when someone doesn't reply to your LINE?"

### Better
"Why do some people interpret other people's reactions as a judgment of their own worth?"

LINE is only a behavioral example used to make the psychological pattern recognizable.

Use:

```text
SITUATION
→ BEHAVIOR
→ PSYCHOLOGICAL PATTERN
→ HIDDEN MECHANISM
→ INNER PROCESS
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ REFRAME
→ INSIGHT
```

The script must always prioritize the psychological pattern over the situation.

---

## 2. Target Style

The target is analytical psychological content similar to PsychToons-style videos.

The script should feel:

- psychological
- observational
- analytical
- direct
- highly relatable
- intellectually clear
- slightly provocative
- concise
- easy to visualize
- focused on "why people behave this way"

The script should NOT feel like:

- a short story
- a fictional narrative
- a diary
- a motivational speech
- therapy dialogue
- a self-help article
- a lecture
- a long personal anecdote
- an essay with excessive transitions

---

## 3. Opening Rule

DO NOT start with a long cinematic scene.

Avoid:

- "夜、布団の中でスマホを開く。"
- "会議室のドアが閉まる。"
- "朝、会社に着くと..."
- "その日の夜、あなたは..."

These create STORY MODE.

Instead, start with psychological recognition.

Preferred structure:

```text
WHY
↓
WHAT TYPE OF PERSON
↓
WHAT PSYCHOLOGICAL PATTERN
```

Examples:

> なぜ、LINEの返信が少し遅いだけで、「嫌われたかもしれない」と考えてしまうのでしょうか。

> 他人の反応を気にしすぎる人には、ある共通した心理パターンがあります。

> 本当に気にしているのは、相手がどう思ったかではありません。「自分がどう評価されたか」なのです。

The first 15–30 seconds must immediately identify the psychological problem and create self-recognition.

---

## 4. Psychological Identity Must Be Clear

Before writing, internally define:

**WHO IS THIS VIDEO ABOUT?**

Do NOT define the person only by the situation.

Bad:

> People who worry when LINE replies are slow.

Good:

> People who use other people's reactions as evidence of their own worth.

The psychological identity should be expressible in one sentence.

The entire script must explain this identity.

---

## 5. Use Behavioral Signs, Not Stories

Use short recognizable examples:

- checking LINE repeatedly
- rereading sent messages
- analyzing emoji usage
- interpreting short replies
- changing wording repeatedly
- apologizing unnecessarily
- checking facial expressions
- replaying conversations
- seeking reassurance

These are behavioral evidence, NOT stories.

Use them as:

> こういう行動をしていませんか？

NOT:

> ある夜、あなたはスマホを手に取り...

Never build a chronological narrative around these examples.

---

## 6. Explain the Hidden Mechanism

Do not stop at:

> あなたは気にしすぎています。

Explain WHY.

Required causal chain:

```text
TRIGGER
↓
INTERPRETATION
↓
EMOTION
↓
URGE
↓
BEHAVIOR
↓
SHORT-TERM RELIEF
↓
LONG-TERM REINFORCEMENT
```

Example:

```text
返信が遅い
↓
「自分が何か悪いことをしたのでは？」
↓
不安
↓
確認したくなる
↓
LINEを何度も開く
↓
一瞬だけ安心する
↓
「返信＝自分の価値」という結びつきが強化される
↓
次回はさらに不安になる
```

This mechanism is more important than the external situation.

---

## 7. Explain the Misinterpretation

Every video should identify a common but incorrect interpretation.

Example:

```text
FACT:
「相手から返信が来ない」

INTERPRETATION:
「自分が嫌われた」
```

Explicitly separate:

**WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED**

vs.

**WHAT THE MIND ADDED**

---

## 8. Introduce the Psychological Framework After the Pattern

Do NOT immediately introduce Adler psychology / 課題の分離.

First make the viewer understand:

> "Ah, this is what my mind is doing."

Then introduce the framework.

Correct order:

```text
Behavior
→ Pattern
→ Mechanism
→ Why it happens
→ Psychological framework
→ Reinterpretation
```

NOT:

```text
Problem
→ 「This is 課題の分離」
→ several paragraphs explaining theory
```

The theory must support the observed psychological pattern, not become the topic.

---

## 9. Avoid Repetitive Theory Explanation

Do not repeatedly say:

- 「これは相手の課題です。」
- 「相手の課題なのです。」
- 「相手の領域です。」
- 「相手の課題に踏み込んでいます。」

Once the concept is established, move forward.

Every paragraph must add a NEW psychological insight.

If a paragraph only restates the previous paragraph, delete or merge it.

---

## 10. Include a Psychological Paradox

Strong scripts should contain a paradox.

Examples:

> 安心するために返信を確認しているのに、確認するほど不安が強くなる。

> 相手に嫌われないように気を遣っているのに、その行動が自分をさらに不安定にしている。

The paradox should reveal why the behavior continues.

---

## 11. Explain Why the Pattern Persists

Do not merely say the behavior is bad.

Explain its reinforcement mechanism:

```text
不安
→ 確認
→ 一時的な安心
→ また不安
→ 再確認
```

The viewer should understand:

> "I keep doing this because the behavior temporarily reduces uncertainty."

That is psychological explanation.

---

## 12. Avoid Generic Comfort Language

Avoid excessive phrases such as:

- 「あなたは壊れていません。」
- 「あなたは弱くありません。」
- 「それはあなたのせいではありません。」
- 「あなたは素晴らしい人です。」
- 「あなたの優しさの証です。」

These may be used sparingly, but NEVER as a substitute for psychological explanation.

Replace generic reassurance with mechanism-based reassurance.

Bad:

> あなたは弱くありません。

Better:

> 問題は感受性の強さではありません。その情報を「相手の状態」ではなく「自分の評価」として処理していることです。

---

## 13. Don't Over-Prescribe

Do not turn the ending into a 5-step self-help tutorial.

Avoid:

```text
Step 1...
Step 2...
Step 3...
Step 4...
Step 5...
```

unless the topic genuinely requires a procedural format.

Prefer 2–3 practical applications embedded naturally into the analysis.

The viewer should first understand:

**WHY**

before being told:

**WHAT TO DO.**

---

## 14. Required Script Flow

Use this default architecture:

### A. Psychological Hook
Immediately identify the psychological pattern.

### B. Self-Recognition
Show 3–5 behavioral signs.

### C. Common Misinterpretation
Explain what people usually think the behavior means.

### D. Reframe
Reveal what is actually happening psychologically.

### E. Hidden Mechanism
Explain the internal causal process.

### F. Reinforcement Loop
Explain why the behavior keeps repeating.

### G. Psychological Depth
Explain the deeper reason behind the pattern.

### H. Psychological Framework
Introduce Adler / CBT / attachment / social psychology / cognitive bias etc. ONLY if genuinely relevant.

### I. Paradox
Show how the attempt to solve the problem actually maintains it.

### J. Reframe / Application
Give a practical way to reinterpret the pattern.

### K. Final Insight
End with one memorable psychological realization.

### L. CTA
Short and natural.

---

## 15. Paragraph Rule

Every paragraph must perform ONE of these functions:

1. identify a behavior
2. explain a psychological mechanism
3. reveal a hidden assumption
4. distinguish fact from interpretation
5. explain reinforcement
6. introduce a psychological concept
7. create a paradox
8. provide a reframe
9. provide practical application

If a paragraph does none of these:

**REMOVE IT.**

---

## 16. Anti-Story Rule

The script MUST NOT develop a chronological fictional narrative.

Maximum acceptable scene description:

**1 short sentence.**

Do NOT create sequences such as:

> 夜 → 翌朝 → 会社に着いて → 会議が始まって → その後 → 家に帰って → ベッドに入って

This creates story progression.

Examples are allowed only as isolated behavioral snapshots.

---

## 17. Anti-Essay Rule

Do not write long academic paragraphs.

Use:

```text
short paragraphs
+
clear psychological transitions
+
concrete behavioral examples
+
causal explanations
```

The viewer should follow the psychological logic without feeling like they are reading an article.

---

## 18. Retention Rule

Every 20–40 seconds, introduce at least one of:

- new psychological insight
- surprising distinction
- paradox
- behavioral pattern
- mechanism
- reframe
- unanswered psychological question

Do NOT maintain retention through fictional suspense.

Maintain retention through:

> "Wait, that's actually why I do this?"

---

## 19. Central Thesis Rule

Before writing, generate:

- CORE PSYCHOLOGICAL IDENTITY
- CORE QUESTION
- CORE MISINTERPRETATION
- CORE MECHANISM
- CORE PARADOX
- CORE REFRAME

Then write the entire script around these.

Do not allow the script to drift into multiple unrelated lessons.

---

## 20. Final Quality Test

Before returning the script, ask:

1. Is the situation merely an example?
2. Is the psychological pattern the real topic?
3. Does the opening create self-recognition immediately?
4. Is there minimal storytelling?
5. Is there a clear psychological identity?
6. Is the causal mechanism explained?
7. Is the reinforcement loop explained?
8. Is fact separated from interpretation?
9. Is the psychological framework supporting the analysis?
10. Is there a paradox?
11. Does every paragraph add new information?
12. Is generic motivational language minimized?
13. Does the ending deliver a psychological insight rather than just encouragement?
14. Does the script feel like an analysis of a type of mind, rather than a story about a person?

If any answer is NO:

**REWRITE the relevant section before output.**

---

## 21. New QA Gate: Psychology Depth Check

Add a dedicated `psychology_depth_check` stage.

This gate must NOT merely detect storytelling.

It must verify whether the script actually contains psychological analysis.

Check:

```text
Does the script explain WHY?

Does it identify a psychological pattern?

Does it distinguish the situation from the psychological pattern?

Does it contain a causal mechanism?

Does it explain the internal process?

Does it explain reinforcement?

Does it contain a meaningful reframe?

Is the psychological framework secondary to the actual psychological pattern?

Is the script still understandable if all story examples are removed?

Does every major section introduce new psychological information?
```

---

## 22. Most Important Diagnostic Test

Ask:

> **"If we remove all scenes and everyday examples from this script, does the psychological analysis still stand on its own?"**

### YES
The script is probably psychology-first.

### NO
The script is still relying on storytelling.

Incorporate this test into QA logic.

---

## 23. Example Transformation

### Story-driven

> 夜、LINEを送ったあと、返信が来ない。あなたはスマホを何度も確認する。送った文章を読み返し、何か悪いことを言ったのではないかと考える。

### Psychology-first

> なぜ、返信が遅いだけで「嫌われたかもしれない」と考えてしまうのでしょうか。
>
> 返信がないという事実と、「自分が嫌われた」という解釈は、本来まったく別のものです。
>
> それでも両者を結びつけてしまう人は、他人の反応を「自分への評価」として処理する傾向があります。
>
> だから返信を確認するたびに、相手の状況を確認しているつもりで、実際には自分の価値を確認しているのです。

The second version is the target direction.

---

## 24. Runtime Architecture Recommendation

Do not put all responsibility on the `writing` stage.

Recommended pipeline:

```text
psychology_brief
        ↓
psychological identity
        ↓
mechanism
        ↓
route
        ↓
script_contract
        ↓
writing
        ↓
anti-story QA
        ↓
psychology-depth QA
        ↓
repair if needed
        ↓
final script
```

Responsibilities:

### `psychology_brief`
Locks:

- psychological identity
- core question
- tension
- mechanism
- causal chain
- inner process
- psychological route

### `script_contract`
Locks:

- psychological spine
- section purpose
- mechanism progression
- retention points
- anti-story constraints

### `writing`
Converts the locked psychological architecture into natural Japanese.

### `structure_check`
Detects:

- excessive scenes
- chronological narrative
- storytelling markers

### `psychology_depth_check`
Detects:

- insufficient psychological explanation
- generic self-help
- situation treated as the topic
- missing mechanism
- missing reinforcement
- missing psychological identity
- repetitive theory
- weak reframe

### `repair`
Repairs the specific failed dimension rather than rewriting randomly.

---

## 25. Final Writer Instruction

The final writer must follow:

> **Do not tell the viewer what happened to someone. Explain what is happening inside the viewer's mind.**

And:

> **The viewer should recognize themselves through behaviors, then understand themselves through psychology.**

The script is successful when the viewer thinks:

> 「これ、まさに自分のことだ。」

followed by:

> 「だから自分はこうしていたのか。」

NOT:

> 「いい話だった。」

---

## Output Requirement

Return ONLY the rewritten script.

Do not explain the architecture.
Do not describe the changes.
Do not add notes.

The final script must feel like:

> **Psychological analysis that happens to use everyday situations.**

NOT:

> **An everyday story with psychology added to it.**
